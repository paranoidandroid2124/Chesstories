An engine line is not an explanation by itself.

If Stockfish prefers one quiet developing move over another by a small margin, the useful question is not only "which move is best?" The useful question is why the position rewards that move. Does it preserve central tension? Does it improve the worst piece? Does it prepare a pawn break? Does it stop the opponent's plan without wasting time?

Chesstory keeps the engine visible because the number protects the explanation from becoming vague. Evaluation, depth, and candidate lines should remain on the page.

But the explanation should do more than restate the number. It should separate:

- the direct effect of the played move
- the difference from the best move
- the strategic idea of the position
- the proof line that supports the claim
- the lesson a player can use in the next game

That is the standard Chesstory is trying to reach.
